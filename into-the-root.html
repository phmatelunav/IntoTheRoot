What is measured

  • 1 scene unit = 1 cm. The scale bar is computed from the camera every frame.
  • Lateral root 0.5 mm wide; root hairs 10 µm wide, 0.2–0.8 mm long2.
  • Soil grains 0.1–0.6 mm: fine to medium sand, the size class roots thread between3.
  • Root growth is compressed (1 s ≈ 1 day) and labelled on screen; the zoom runs in real time.
  • Water flow follows a line sink: r² = r₀² − 2kt, so speed rises as 1/r near the root4. 24,000 parcels, computed on the GPU.
  • Capillary bridges join grains that nearly touch, and grains that touch the root.
  • Drought slider: the largest water-filled pore follows Young–Laplace, r = 2γ/|ψ|; conductivity follows van Genuchten–Mualem for a loam (α = 0.036 cm⁻¹, n = 1.56). 3,5,6,7 Particle speed shows K¼ so a millionfold drop stays visible.
  • Mycorrhizal hyphae are 5 µm wide and branch along the soil–glass interface, as in rhizobox imaging; spores are 90–180 µm14.

What is accurate

  • Shot 1 is a Yoshino cherry (Prunus × yedoensis): flowers open before the leaves, five notched petals, umbels of 2–5 on long pedicels29. About 25,000 flowers and tens of thousands of roots in four branching orders.
  • The section is a tetrarch dicot root: exarch xylem with protoxylem at the four poles, wide metaxylem in the centre, phloem between the arms8.
  • Casparian strips sit mid-way along the radial walls of the endodermis. Cortex cells hold starch and leave air spaces at their corners9,10.
  • Colours follow a safranin / fast-green stain: lignin red, cellulose green11.
  • Shot 6 builds the root as about 5,000 irregular 3D cells in seven layers, each with a visible wall (drawn about 3× real thickness so it reads on screen). Arbuscules form only in the inner cortex next to the endodermis; host cells there lose their starch and their nucleus moves to the centre and enlarges.
  • The Casparian strip is drawn as it is in 3D: a lignified belt around each endodermal cell, not a dot.
  • Shot 7 runs in its own nanometre-scale scene. The bilayer is about 4 nm thick with 18% phytosterols. Aquaporins are tetramers, each monomer with six membrane helices and two half-helices meeting at the NPA motif; waters point their hydrogens away from it, the orientation that blocks proton hopping. The H⁺-ATPase has 10 membrane helices plus N, P and A domains; the nitrate symporter has 12 helices in two rocking halves. Proteins are shown as helix cylinders, not atomic surfaces; bulk water is thinned to about 5% so the scene stays readable.

Scientific basis, shot by shot

Every size, rate and structure on screen comes from the literature below. Where the animation compresses time or simplifies geometry, the on-screen labels say so.

Shot 1 · A flowering cherry

The tree is a Yoshino cherry, Prunus × yedoensis, which blooms before its leaves emerge; its flowers have five notched pale-pink petals and hang in umbels of two to five on long pedicels29. Cherry bark is glossy and marked by horizontal lenticels. Like most fruit trees, cherries root shallowly: most woody roots lie in the top 60 cm and spread beyond the crown, with a few sinker roots going deeper30,31. The roots branch through four orders down to absorptive fine roots under 1 mm in diameter, the ones that carry root hairs and mycorrhizal partners32. Root growth is compressed to about five years per second. With the soil made transparent, the root system is shown as excavation or X-ray tomography reveals it1; Prunus forms arbuscular mycorrhizas, which is why shots 5 and 6 show that partnership14.

Shot 2 · Root, hairs and soil water

The lateral root is 0.5 mm wide. Root hairs are tip-growing outgrowths of single epidermal cells, about 10 µm wide and up to roughly 1 mm long2. The grains are 0.1–0.6 mm, the fine-to-medium sand class3. Water is held between grains by capillarity, so the widest pore that stays water-filled at a given matric potential ψ follows the Young–Laplace relation3:

r = 2γ cos θ / |ψ| (γ = 0.0728 N m⁻¹, θ ≈ 0)

Water converging on a single root behaves as a line sink, so its speed rises as 1/r near the root surface4. Relative hydraulic conductivity under the drought slider follows the van Genuchten–Mualem model5,6 with loam parameters (α = 0.036 cm⁻¹, n = 1.56)7:

Sₑ = [1 + (α h)ⁿ]^(−m), m = 1 − 1/n; K/Kₛ = Sₑ^½ [1 − (1 − Sₑ^(1/m))^m]²

Shots 3–4 · The cut and the stained section

The section is a tetrarch dicot root: exarch xylem with protoxylem at four poles, wide metaxylem in the centre and phloem strands alternating with the xylem arms8. The Casparian strip is a lignin polymer deposited in the radial and transverse walls of the endodermis9,10. Colours follow the safranin / fast-green double stain, lignin red and cellulose green11. Water moves inward along apoplastic, symplastic and transcellular routes until the Casparian strip forces it across a plasma membrane12.

Shot 5 · Fungal partners

Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi associate with about 72% of vascular plant species13. Their hyphae are a few micrometres wide and reach beyond the phosphate depletion zone; spores range from about 40 to 800 µm14. The plant pays in sugars and in fatty acids that the fungus cannot make itself15,16.

Shot 6 · Inside the cortex

Arbuscules form in inner cortical cells next to the endodermis. Each is enclosed by a plant-made periarbuscular membrane; the host cell's nucleus moves to the centre and its starch is depleted17. Individual arbuscules collapse after a few days and are replaced18. The Casparian strip is drawn as the continuous belt it forms around each endodermal cell10.

Shot 7 · The membrane

Plant plasma membranes are rich in phytosterols, which order the bilayer19. Aquaporins are tetramers; each monomer has six membrane helices and two half-helices that meet at the NPA motifs in the middle of the pore20,21. Water crosses in single file, and its dipole flips at the NPA region, which prevents proton hopping22; one channel passes on the order of 3 × 10⁹ water molecules per second23. The plasma-membrane H⁺-ATPase has ten membrane helices and cytosolic N, P and A domains24, and moves one proton per ATP to build a membrane potential of about −120 to −200 mV12,25. The nitrate transporter NRT1.1 has twelve membrane helices in two halves that rock between outward- and inward-open states26,27, coupling nitrate uptake to the proton gradient28.
